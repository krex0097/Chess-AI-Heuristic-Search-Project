🌲 1. Depth-First Search (DFS) & Game Trees

A two-player adversarial chess game can be modeled as a directed game tree where the current board is the root, directed edges represent legal chess moves, and child nodes represent resulting game states.

Why DFS? Chess has an average branching factor of $b \approx 35$. Breadth-First Search (BFS) requires storing an entire depth level in memory ($O(b^d)$ space), exhausting RAM almost instantly. DFS requires only $O(b \times d)$ space, storing only the active path from root to leaf along with unexplored siblings on the recursion stack.

⏱️ 2. The Combinatorial Horizon & Depth Limits

Shannon calculated that a full game of chess contains roughly $10^{120}$ possible positions (Shannon's Number). Even exploring 5 full plies requires evaluating over $52 \text{ million}$ hypothetical states.

The Depth Limit: Because the tree cannot be searched to terminal checkmate within practical time, we enforce a depth limit ($d$). When $d = 0$, recursion terminates, and we invoke a static heuristic evaluation function to estimate the relative positional strength.

⚖️ 3. Minimax Decision Rule

Minimax assumes zero-sum play under perfect adversarial rationality:

  • Maximizing Player (White): Chooses the candidate move yielding the highest evaluation score ($\max$).
  • Minimizing Player (Black): Predicted to choose the countermove yielding the lowest evaluation score ($\min$).

Scores are backed up recursively from leaf evaluation nodes back to the root, guaranteeing the AI selects the move that maximizes its guaranteed outcome regardless of the opponent's best reply.

✂️ 4. Alpha-Beta Pruning Optimization

Alpha-Beta pruning preserves the exact same minimax decision while eliminating branches that cannot possibly affect the root choice:

  • $\alpha$: The minimum score White is already guaranteed to achieve.
  • $\beta$: The maximum score Black is already guaranteed to achieve.

Whenever $\beta \le \alpha$, a cutoff occurs: the current branch is abandoned because the opponent already has a better alternative elsewhere. In optimal move ordering, Alpha-Beta cuts the effective branching factor from $b$ down to $\sqrt{b}$, effectively doubling the searchable depth in the same runtime.

🧮 5. Heuristic Evaluation Components

Our project implements an explainable, multi-factor additive evaluation function:

  • Material: Pawn = 100, Knight = 320, Bishop = 330, Rook = 500, Queen = 900.
  • Piece-Square Tables (PST): Encourages piece development, centralization of knights, and castling safety.
  • Center Control: Rewards control of $\{d4, e4, d5, e5\}$ and outer boundary squares.
  • Piece Mobility: Incentivizes open diagonals and active files.
  • King Safety: Penalizes open king files and missing shield pawns in castled structures.
  • Checkmate: Large special constant ($100,000 \pm d$) ensuring checkmate overrides all material calculations.

🎯 6. Why Non-Material Winning Moves are Chosen

Undergraduate examiners often ask: "Why did the AI choose a quiet move instead of capturing a free pawn?"

Minimax looks ahead $d$ plies. If capturing a pawn falls into an opponent tactical trap (e.g., pinning the queen or allowing a checkmate net 2 plies later), the backed-up minimax score for the capture will be severely negative. The AI will correctly decline immediate material gain to defend or achieve a superior strategic advantage.
